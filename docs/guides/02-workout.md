# Training Plan — Build, then Engine, then Match Fit

**The long-term goal:** add muscle first, then build the conditioning to play at least 60 minutes of a full 11v11 adult soccer match.

| Phase | When | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Build | 8 weeks, Nov 16 – Jan 10 | Muscle. 3 home strength sessions a week. No running, no jump rope |
| 2. Engine | Weeks 9–16 (as UPS ends) | Keep the muscle (2 strength sessions), add 3 conditioning days |
| 3. Match fit | Week 17 onward | Soccer-specific intervals and games, up to a tested 60 minutes |

Realistic target for Phase 1, with hard eating: about **4–8 lb** (149 → roughly 155 lb). That is visibly more muscle. "Big" takes longer, and Phase 2 keeps building while you get fit.

## Phase 1: Build

**Schedule:** A on Thursday 9:00 AM · B on Saturday 9:00 AM · C on Sunday 7:00 PM. About 45 minutes each, at home with dumbbells, bands and a mat. The calendar reminds you 15 minutes before, and the app's Workout tab picks the right session for the day.

**How hard:** slow, controlled reps, no jumping. Every set should end with 1–2 good reps left. Easy sets don't build muscle; grinding to failure isn't needed either. Rest 90 seconds to 2 minutes between sets.

**Warm-up (3 min):** 10 bodyweight squats, 10 arm circles each way, one light set of the first exercise.

### Isometric holds (the last two moves of each workout)

An isometric hold means getting into a position and staying still while the muscle works. They're placed at the end so they never tire you before the main lifts, and most are held where the muscle is stretched (bottom of a split squat or push-up), which is the position where holds best support muscle growth. They also build tendon strength, which matters for sprinting, jumping and contact.

- **Copenhagen plank** strengthens the inner thighs; it's one of the best-studied exercises for preventing groin injuries in soccer.
- **Single-leg glute bridge hold** strengthens the hamstrings and glutes, the muscles most often strained when sprinting.
- **Farmer's hold** builds grip, traps and core bracing for contact.

How: about 8/10 effort, perfectly still, steady breathing (never hold your breath). Log seconds in the Reps box. Progress by adding 5–10 seconds, then weight. Holds add to the lifts; they don't replace them.

The Neck project already uses isometric holds, and the Calves project ends with a single-leg calf raise hold (2 × 30 s each leg) for Achilles tendon strength.

### How to progress

1. Start with a weight you can do for the **bottom** of the rep range.
2. Each session, try to add a rep.
3. When **every set hits the top** of the range, go up 5 lb and start again at the bottom.
4. The app shows what you did last time, so you always know what to beat.

### Recovery rules

- **UPS is already hard physical work.** If a shift wrecked you, do 2 sets instead of 3. Showing up counts.
- **Slept under 5 hours, or resting heart rate 5+ bpm above normal?** 2 sets per exercise, or just the morning ritual.
- **Missed a session?** Don't double up. Do the next one on the schedule.
- **Eat.** Training only sends the signal; food builds the muscle. If the weekly average weight isn't rising, eat more.
- If your weight keeps dropping for 2–3 weeks despite eating more, see a doctor.

## Stretching (5 minutes a day)

The app's **Stretch** tab has three guided routines. Press Start and a timer walks you through each stretch: 5 seconds to get into position, then a countdown, left and right sides separately. The minutes fill the **Flex** bar (goal: 5 a day).

| Routine | Length | When | Stretches |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wake-up | 5 min | In the morning ritual | Cat-cow, child's pose, world's greatest stretch, downward dog, thread the needle |
| Legs + hips | 6 min | After every workout | Half-kneeling hip flexor, lying hamstring, figure-four, standing calf, butterfly |
| Unwind | 5 min | After UPS, before bed | Child's pose, doorway chest, supine spinal twist, figure-four, cat-cow |

Rules: ease in until you feel a pull, never pain. Keep breathing. Don't bounce.

## Animated demos and guided sets

Every exercise and stretch has a **▶ Demo** button. It opens a 3D figure that performs the move on a loop: the working muscles are lit in green, dumbbells in teal, bands in yellow, and a one-line cue changes as the move goes. Drag sideways on the figure to look at the move from another angle. The 3D model and the Three.js library load from a free public CDN the first time you open a demo; if they cannot load, a simpler drawn figure shows instead.

Below the figure, **Start guided set** runs the set for you:

- 5 seconds to get into position, then a start tone.
- Rep moves: the counter goes up each time the figure finishes a rep, with a soft tick. A higher blip means you reached the bottom of the rep range; the set ends at the top of the range, or tap **Set done**.
- Holds: a countdown with ticks on 3, 2, 1 and a chime at the end.
- Moves done on each side get a "Switch sides" pause.
- Between sets a rest timer runs (75 s for rep moves, 45 s for holds) with ticks before the next set.

The figure sets a steady tempo of about 4 seconds a rep. That is deliberate: slow reps build more control. **Watch a video instead** opens a real person doing the move on YouTube; the video IDs live in one list in `src/Stretch.js`.

## Projects (add-on sessions)

Short sessions for specific goals. They log like any workout but don't count toward the 3 main workouts a week and don't change which workout is next. Find them under **Projects** in the Workout tab (Neck, Calves) and the Stretch tab (Hip mobility). Every move has an animated ▶ Demo.

**Neck strength** (3× a week, ~8 min, right after Workouts A, B and C). Why: a strong neck supports the spine and helps absorb contact and headers.
- Supine chin tuck 2 × 10, hold 3 s
- Neck isometric hold, hand resistance: 2 rounds × 10 s each way (front, back, left, right)
- Prone neck extension 2 × 12, slow
- Side-lying neck raise 2 × 12 each side
- Dumbbell shrug 3 × 12–15
- Slow and controlled, no jerking. Start with no extra weight. Stop for sharp pain, numbness, tingling or a headache.

**Calf size** (3× a week on rest days: Mon, Wed, Fri, ~10 min). Why: the calves are strong but small; size comes from more weekly sets, a deep stretch at the bottom and steady added weight.
- Single-leg deficit calf raise on a step, holding a dumbbell: 3 × 10–15 each leg
- Seated dumbbell calf raise (soleus): 3 × 15–20
- Tibialis raise against a wall: 2 × 15–20
- Single-leg calf raise hold, halfway up: 2 × 30 s each leg
- Every rep: 3 s down, 2 s pause in the stretch, up and squeeze 1 s. Add weight when every set hits the top of the range. Size also needs the calorie surplus.

**Hip mobility** (Stretch tab, 8 min, daily for 4 weeks, then 4× a week). Why: tight hips limit squat depth, stride and kicking range, and pull on the lower back.
- 90/90 hip switch 60 s · Hip CARs 30 s each side · Half-kneeling hip flexor 45 s each side · Pigeon pose 45 s each side · Frog stretch 60 s · Deep squat hold 60 s · Cossack squat 60 s
- Counts toward the Flex bar.

## Phase 2: Engine (outline, detailed when you get there)

- **Strength:** 2 sessions a week (one full body, one legs) to hold muscle.
- **Conditioning:** 3 days. Weeks 9–12: easy 20–30 minute runs and jump rope to build a base. Weeks 13–16: intervals (for example 4 × 4 minutes hard, 3 minutes easy), short sprints, and ball work.
- No "cut": at your size there's nothing to cut. Conditioning leans you out on its own, so keep eating at maintenance or slightly above.

## Phase 3: Match fit (outline)

- Soccer-shaped intervals: repeated sprints, changes of direction, small-sided games.
- Test: build from 2 × 20 minutes of match play to 60 minutes of 11v11.
- Strength stays at 1–2 short sessions a week to protect knees, hamstrings and ankles.

## Checkpoint (around Dec 13, week 4)

In your weekly review, check: weekly average weight rising 0.5–1 lb a week, at least 9 of 12 sessions done, lifts going up. If weight is flat, add 200–300 kcal a day. If sessions are being missed, tell Claude and we'll reshape the schedule.
